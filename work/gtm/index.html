Monkeys Leave Behind

A go-to-market strategy for Edulis, a hair color line for women who have run out of good options.

Client
Stealth startup, Berkeley
Role
Go-to-market intern
Year
2025
Output
Persona research, channel strategy, 12-month plan

The problem

Cover the gray, or keep the hair

Gray reveals an age many women are not ready to hand over, so they color it, knowing what the chemicals do. In their eyes it is a straight trade, and nobody has offered them a third option.

70%

of women past 40 dye their hair regularly

Who decides

Two people, one purchase

The buyer

Menopausal Middle-Aged Mary Anne

I know my hair is being damaged, but I have no other option.

Navigating menopause, overwhelmed and under-informed. Her hair is the daily reminder. She wants a product that works and information she can trust.

The gatekeeper

Curious, Cautious Kim

For older women, we don't have a choice but to damage their hair.

25 years behind the chair. Her reputation rides on every result, so she will not gamble on a product she cannot explain to the person in her chair.

What stylists said

Four ways of asking the same thing

  • I am a chemist.
  • Every product I see causes damage.
  • I don't like being sold to.
  • I need to touch a product before there is any chance I sell it.
  • It's important to let stylists try a product out.
  • If you just make a sales pitch, I wouldn't buy it.

The insight

Kim is not resistant. She is unconvinced.

Every one of those quotes is a request to learn something, not a refusal to buy. Edulis does not have a product objection to overcome; it has a teaching problem.

Education is the channel.

Belief model from stylist interviews
