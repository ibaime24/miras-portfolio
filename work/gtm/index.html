STEALTH STARTUP · GO-TO-MARKET · BERKELEY 2025

Monkeys Leave Behind

A go-to-market strategy for Edulis, a hair color line for women who have run out of good options. The buyer is the woman in the chair. The gatekeeper is the stylist holding the bowl.

70% of women over 40 dye their hair regularly

2 decision makers, one purchase

0 products they trust to do both jobs

THE TRADE-OFF

Cover the gray, or keep the hair

Gray hair reveals an age many women are not ready to hand over. So they dye it, knowing what the chemicals do. In their eyes it is a straight trade: cover the grays, or accept the damage. It is a question about identity, and it is a question about health, and right now those two things pull in opposite directions.

THE BUYER

Menopausal Middle-Aged Mary Anne

I know my hair is being damaged, but I have no other option.

She is navigating menopause and a body that keeps changing on her. She feels overwhelmed and under-informed, and her hair is the daily reminder. She wants a product that works and information she can trust. She wants to feel vibrant and alive.

All I can think about right now is all the ways my body is changing. I'm trying to learn anything and everything I can.

THE GATEKEEPER

Curious, Cautious Kim

For older women, we don't have a choice but to damage their hair.

Her reputation and her livelihood ride on every result. She wants to innovate and she cannot afford to guess. Every brand on the shelf says healthy and organic, and she has heard it before.

We have a lot at stake here; we aren't just going to try any old product. It's all just chemistry. I'm a chemist, but it's tough to explain to clients.

THE INSIGHT

Kim is not resistant. She is unconvinced.

Mary Anne will not switch to a product her colorist will not use, and Kim will not pick up a product she cannot explain. She is already a chemist; what she lacks is a way to make the chemistry legible to the person in her chair. That reframes the problem. Edulis does not have a product objection to overcome. It has a teaching problem.

Education is the lever. Everything else is downstream of it.

THE STRATEGY

Teach the trade, then sell to it

NOW

Continuing education for working stylists

Stylists already retrain constantly, and not only in schools. Ongoing programs and resources give the people holding the bowl today a reason and a method to move toward healthier practice.

LATER

Edulis cosmetology schools

Shape the next generation from the first day of training, with hair health built into the curriculum instead of bolted on. The graduates arrive already fluent.

The same lever answers problems the industry already has. Cosmetology education is expensive and passing rates are low. Stylists are dissatisfied with the products they are handed. And they carry real chemical exposure through every working day. A curriculum built around hair health is not only a channel to market; it is the thing the trade is asking for.

THE LAUNCH

Where the trade already gathers

  • Conventions Barbercon, Cosmoprof Worldwide, and CES, where stylists come specifically to evaluate what is new.
  • Runway Put Edulis in the hands of influential stylists and brands, and let the product demonstrate itself under real conditions.
  • Community Work with voices the trade already trusts, like Angela of Trace Beauty, a Cantu brand partner, inside salon events rather than around them.
